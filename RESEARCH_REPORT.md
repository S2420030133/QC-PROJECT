# Research report

## 1. Question and defensible claim

This computational study asks whether a separate labeled calibration partition can recover binary classification when simulated noise shifts variational-quantum-classifier scores, and how the result compares with linear and quadratic zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE) under a finite measurement budget.

The supported claim is narrow: **in the tested simulated settings, a fixed zero threshold can produce single-class predictions even while the score retains class-ranking information; a threshold chosen using separate calibration labels can recover balanced accuracy, while finite-shot precision and the calibration budget affect that recovery.** Calibration also improves the clean zero-noise baseline, so its noisy-setting gain is not all noise recovery. The experiments do not establish hardware behavior, a universal method ranking, clinical validity, quantum advantage, or generalization to new populations.

The related-work section includes classifier threshold selection, probability calibration, quantum-classifier noise robustness, foundational and recent ZNE literature, finite-shot studies, and budget-aware mitigation work. The contribution is framed narrowly as an operational comparison of label-dependent threshold fitting and label-free probability-scaled ZNE under matched total measurement costs; no new estimator or general mitigation principle is claimed.

## 2. Data and implementation

The four tasks are WDBC (569 samples, 30 features), Wine class 0 versus classes 1 and 2 (178 samples, 13 features), and balanced and imbalanced synthetic binary data (600 samples each, 12 features). Five stratified outer folds have disjoint test sets and cover each example once. Each fold separates model fitting, calibration, and test data; preprocessing excludes calibration and test partitions. Quantum models are noiselessly trained for 30 epochs using Adam, then evaluated with exact expectations and PennyLane's mixed-state simulator with sampled shots. The noise probabilities are synthetic per-channel-location probabilities, not measured device error rates or physical (T_1/T_2) values.

The study contains 56 model configurations, including two-qubit task models and architecture controls. The main matched finite-shot comparisons cover WDBC, Wine, balanced synthetic data, and a feature-probed four-qubit WDBC CNOT-star. The imbalanced synthetic task remains a negative result. The manuscript distinguishes observable-estimation error from classification recovery and labels the clean-frozen threshold a simulator-only reference.

## 3. Primary standardized WDBC comparison

The primary matched-budget WDBC analysis standardizes features and fits two-component PCA using each outer training pool, retaining 0.634 of variance. At composite noise (gamma=0.30), reconciled exact threshold selection reaches 0.878 balanced accuracy; calibrated exact linear and quadratic ZNE reach 0.883 and 0.884. At 1,000 nominal measurements per test case, split between calibration and inference, noisy threshold selection averages 0.778, linear ZNE plus calibration 0.716, and quadratic ZNE plus calibration 0.582. These finite-shot means average 20 shot draws within each of 10 saved checkpoints. The figure and table SDs are across the 10 checkpoint means, nested in five folds whose training sets share observations. Fixed threshold yields 0.503.

**Exact-result reconciliation.** A prior saved standardized WDBC summary reported mean exact noisy-calibrated balanced accuracy 0.891. Recomputing the exact-noise grid from the saved checkpoint weights, preprocessing objects, split indices, and per-example scores found that 10 model-row thresholds and several balanced-accuracy values differed. The recomputed scores are saved in `sensitivity/wdbc_standardized_exact_noise_sweep_observations.csv`; before/after values are recorded in `sensitivity/wdbc_standardized_exact_metric_reconciliation.json`. The corrected mean is 0.878 (SD 0.027; AUC 0.958). The cause of the previous discrepancy is not established. The paper, abstract, figure, and summary now use 0.878.

Paired noisy-threshold-minus-ZNE differences averaged within each outer fold are +0.071, +0.071, +0.052, +0.052, +0.065 for linear ZNE and +0.210, +0.199, +0.200, +0.191, +0.179 for quadratic ZNE. Test folds partition the data, but training sets share observations; these are descriptive protocol differences, not five independent populations. Shot repeats quantify measurement variability conditional on the saved checkpoints. At clean evaluation, the quantum model reaches 0.887, while logistic regression on the same standardized two inputs reaches 0.928. The matched classical baseline is stronger, so the result does not support a quantum predictive advantage. The fixed threshold has exact AUC 0.958 despite balanced accuracy 0.500, demonstrating the score-ordering/decision-boundary distinction.

A post-hoc calibration-size sweep reuses the 20 saved test-shot draws and compares 10, 20, 50, and 114 calibration labels under the same nominal total-shot budget. For noisy threshold selection, mean balanced accuracy rises from 0.760 at 10 labels to 0.783 at all 114; linear ZNE plus calibration ranges from 0.709 to 0.716, while quadratic ZNE remains near 0.591. These are exploratory results conditional on the same 10 checkpoints and shared training data. The exact selected calibration indices, counts, thresholds, test metrics, and deterministic seed rule are saved in the sweep outputs and script.

A matched 1/2/4-layer diagnostic uses the same standardized folds and initialization seeds. Clean fixed-threshold balanced accuracy is 0.873, 0.887, and 0.885; exact noisy calibrated balanced accuracy is 0.849, 0.878, and 0.873; finite-shot values are 0.854, 0.778, and 0.525. On the held-out calibration set, the noisy class-mean gaps are 0.210, 0.079, and 0.008, while the estimated single-score shot SE at 500 shots is approximately 0.043 at each depth. This pattern is consistent with finite-shot uncertainty overwhelming the depth-4 separation in this circuit, but does not establish a general depth law. The one- and four-layer weights, training histories, score-spread tables, and metric audits are saved.

To examine task transfer, standardized-PCA two-qubit models were trained separately on Wine and balanced synthetic data, using the same saved outer splits and initialization seeds, and evaluated over 20 finite-shot draws per checkpoint at the same noise setting and nominal budget. On Wine, noisy threshold selection reached 0.701 balanced accuracy (SD 0.090), versus 0.650 for calibrated linear ZNE and 0.557 for calibrated quadratic ZNE; clean matched-input logistic regression reached 0.950. On balanced synthetic data, the corresponding values were 0.612 (SD 0.045), 0.584, and 0.534, while clean logistic regression reached 0.747. The 2,400 finite-shot metric rows recompute from saved calibration/test scale observations with zero mismatches; maximum relative budget deviation was 0.0115%. These are fresh model fits but reuse the existing data and outer folds, so they are transfer diagnostics, not independent population replications. Full scores, classical results, checkpoints, and training curves are saved under `sensitivity/standardized_transfer_*`.

The full noise-grid and cross-task results remain available as secondary analyses using the earlier unstandardized PCA pipeline; the main manuscript and supplement label this distinction. A fresh 30-dataset synthetic diagnostic is near chance on its fixed baseline and is not confirmatory evidence for WDBC.

## 4. Secondary unstandardized-PCA classical controls and transfer results

The earlier multi-task pilot used unstandardized PCA. In that pipeline, a newly calculated logistic baseline uses the same two PCA features **and the exact fit examples used by each corresponding quantum model**. Its mean clean balanced accuracy is 0.902 on WDBC, 0.909 on Wine, 0.782 on balanced synthetic data, and 0.502 on imbalanced synthetic data, versus quantum values 0.812, 0.850, 0.697, and 0.526. Paired quantum-minus-classical differences averaged over fold means are −0.089 for WDBC (descriptive interval [−0.118, −0.064]), −0.060 for Wine ([−0.136, 0.003]), −0.085 for balanced synthetic ([−0.127, −0.048]), and +0.024 for imbalanced synthetic ([−0.034, 0.078]). These intervals describe fold variability; the folds share training data. The matched baseline does not support a quantum predictive advantage.

The paper now reports direct paired calibration-versus-ZNE results on the additional finite-shot settings at (gamma=0.30) and 1,000 shots. The mean linear-ZNE-minus-calibration differences are −0.057 on Wine, −0.014 on balanced synthetic, and −0.006 on the four-qubit CNOT-star; the star interval includes zero. These comparisons broaden the pilot but do not establish generalization beyond the tasks and circuits tested.

## 5. Sensitivity and limitations

The calibration allocation sensitivity tests 25%, 50%, and 75% of the nominal 1,000-shot budget for calibration. Noisy-threshold balanced accuracy declines from about 0.762 to 0.711 as the calibration share rises; linear-ZNE-plus-calibration also declines. Consequently, the 50:50 allocation is a design choice, not an established optimum. Exact-expectation ZNE results also vary with the probability-scale schedule; the paper reports the tested alternatives as exploratory analyses on reused test folds.

The new bounded-score diagnostic reconstructs calibrated ZNE estimates from saved scale measurements, clips scores to the physical Pauli-(Z) interval ([-1,1]), and refits thresholds on calibration labels only. At WDBC, (gamma=0.30), and 1,000 shots, 17.5% of quadratic-ZNE test scores lie outside the interval; clipping leaves the mean balanced accuracy essentially unchanged at this particular setting. At other tasks and budgets it changes some metrics. The same samples and shot totals are used, so this is a post-processing sensitivity rather than a new quantum measurement pipeline.

The 4-qubit star passes a feature-to-readout dependence probe, but its predictive performance is near chance; the 4- and 6-qubit forward-chain controls do not establish useful scaling. The synthetic imbalanced task has little clean discrimination. Training histories are now available for every fresh model, but a fixed 30-epoch run is not proof of optimizer convergence. Five outer folds provide limited uncertainty resolution. The channel controls use different noise-location counts and support no causal claim about which channel causes collapse.

## 6. Fresh split-and-seed confirmation

A preregistered confirmation run was performed on 2026-10-16 using five new stratified WDBC test folds and two newly trained initializations per fold. It used standardized two-component PCA, the same two-qubit/two-layer CNOT-chain and composite simulator setting, and 20 fresh finite-shot draws per checkpoint. It reused the public WDBC dataset, so it is a fresh split/seed confirmation, not independent clinical or external-population validation.

At gamma=0.30 and a nominal 1,000 measurements per test case including calibration, calibrated threshold selection reached 0.790 balanced accuracy (ROC-AUC 0.874); calibrated linear ZNE reached 0.721 (0.808), and calibrated quadratic ZNE reached 0.596 (0.645). The exact-expectation balanced accuracies were 0.901, 0.902, and 0.900, respectively. Threshold-minus-linear-ZNE fold differences were +0.075, +0.068, +0.063, +0.060, and +0.081; threshold-minus-quadratic-ZNE differences were +0.195, +0.202, +0.185, +0.196, and +0.193. These are descriptive fold contrasts, not independent-population confidence intervals.

The frozen protocol, split IDs, training curves, weights, preprocessing objects, exact and finite-shot metrics, per-example calibration/test observations, and hashes are saved in `results/runs/independent-confirmation-wdbc-20261016/`. Its separate audit matched all 41 hashed artifacts, verified five train/calibration/test disjointness checks and one-time test coverage, and reconstructed 600 finite-shot and 30 exact metric rows with zero mismatches. This internal audit is not itself a full re-execution. The confirmation reinforces the tested WDBC simulator result; it does not establish external validity, hardware performance, a clinical claim, or a general method ranking.
